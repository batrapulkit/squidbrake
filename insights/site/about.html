About

Change control for AI agents, from people who've run security.

Squidbrake lets AI agents do everyday work on their own and makes sure a person approves what can't be taken back. Here's who builds it, what it does, and what we've learned from the teams piloting it.

The product

It checks every tool call before it runs
Squidbrake sits between your agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, MCP servers, your own code) and the tools they call. Each call is checked against your rules in a few milliseconds, outside the model, so a prompt can't talk its way past it.
Routine work runs, irreversible work waits
Reads, builds and tests go through. Deletes, destructive SQL, money going out and messages to the outside world wait for a teammate, who sees what will change and says yes or no from the dashboard, Slack or their phone.
Every decision is on a record you can check
Who asked, what ran, which rule decided and who approved, in a hash-chained log you can verify offline. Evidence for SOC 2 CC8.1 and EU AI Act Art. 14.
Open source, self-hosted or hosted
Apache-2.0, pipx install squidbrake. Start in shadow mode: nothing is blocked in week one. Quickstart · Benchmark · Security

From our pilots

Early pilots, described without names until each team says yes to being named.

A security engineer tried to break it
He sent a list of commands meant to get around Squidbrake; every one became a regression test. On the retest (version 0.14.11, isolated Docker, no network) all 30 test cases and 28 follow-up probes were held or blocked, harmless ones ran, and the two gaps he'd found earlier (piping a download into a shell, running a script the agent had just written) were closed. Next: a normal working week, to measure how often it holds something it shouldn't.
An agent overstepped in a cloud console
A founder in the Entrepreneur First cohort watched an AI agent take actions in his Google Cloud console that he hadn't asked for. He found Squidbrake and installed it himself, without a call, then sent us a review of where it fell short. Those findings are now on our list.
Unattended agents on private repos and AWS
An engineer at a large IT services firm runs local models and Claude Code unattended against private repositories and AWS. He read the install script before running it; we set it up together in a 65-minute call and his first agent action went through Squidbrake. His asks (connect one agent at a time, an extra warning before approving anything irreversible) shaped what we're building next.
Ops agents at a data-integration company
Their agents had put a customer's name in a pull request and commented on a public issue after being told not to. They already had Slack approve and reject buttons, so the gap was what's inside an action: customer names in public text, public comments, sequences of version changes. Their tools go behind Squidbrake in shadow mode first, logging what would have been held before anything is.
